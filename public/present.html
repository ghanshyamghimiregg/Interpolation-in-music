Overview

Draw a curve through a few notes. Hear it, don't just read it.

Place control points on a pitch-time grid, pick an interpolation method, and the curve plays back as a real instrument glide — so the difference between Linear, Lagrange, and Cubic Spline is something you hear, not just see.

6 control points · three curves on the same notes · click a tab to isolate one

Active formula

Piecewise linear

Connects consecutive points with straight segments. First derivative jumps at every knot — audibly "robotic."

y(x) = yᵢ + (yᵢ₊₁−yᵢ)/(xᵢ₊₁−xᵢ) × (x−xᵢ)

Motivation

Why turn interpolation curves into sound?

Interpolation is normally taught as a static picture — a polynomial, an error number, an oscillating line. Pitch is frequency, frequency is logarithmic, and a glide between two notes is exactly the continuous function that interpolation computes. So the question stops being "which method has lower error?" and becomes "which one sounds like a real musician?"

What I had to solve

Six problems, six fixes

Runge's phenomenon felt abstract

Lagrange curves through many points overshoot wildly, but a graph alone doesn't make that feel like a real problem.

Fix → you hear the pitch shoot to extremes

"Smooth on a graph" ≠ smooth to the ear

Linear interpolation looks fine plotted, but a glide from straight segments sounds like a machine snapping direction at every knot.

Fix → spline glide sounds like a real portamento

Newton forward needs equal spacing

Students forget this constraint and wonder why their difference table breaks on unevenly placed notes.

Fix → app auto-detects spacing; falls back to divided differences

Oscillator synths sounded fake

No amount of ADSR or formant tuning stopped every instrument from sounding like the same synthetic blip.

Fix → switched to real recorded FluidR3_GM samples

A metronome-like clicking crept in

Noise-burst attack transients on every note onset lined up rhythmically into a phantom click track.

Fix → removed the noise bus; sample's natural attack is enough

All instruments blurred into one blip

Reducing sustain uniformly collapsed piano, violin, and flute into the same short envelope — indistinguishable.

Fix → identity comes from the recording, not the envelope

Fundamental math

Why pitch must be logarithmic

Human pitch perception is logarithmic — each octave doubles the frequency. Interpolating raw Hz values creates uneven-feeling glides. The app works entirely in MIDI space (linear semitones) and converts to Hz only for audio playback.

MIDI → frequency (A4 = 440 Hz, MIDI 69) f(midi) = 440 × 2^((midi − 69) / 12)
Frequency → MIDI (inverse) midi(f) = 69 + 12 × log₂(f / 440)

Consequence: interpolating in MIDI means equal pitch steps sound equal — a glide from C3 to C4 feels the same "distance" as C4 to C5, which is exactly how musicians experience pitch.

WebAudioFont slide delta (semitones) Δ = midi(f_target) − midi(f_start)  →  fed as pitch delta to sample engine

The mathematics

Same six notes, three distinct sounds

Lagrange, Newton divided differences, and Newton forward difference all build the same single polynomial through the points — they differ only in how they compute it. So there are really three audibly distinct shapes:

Linear (grey) · Lagrange / Newton family (red) · Natural cubic spline (green) — same 6 points
Linear y(x) = yᵢ + (yᵢ₊₁−yᵢ)/(xᵢ₊₁−xᵢ) × (x−xᵢ),    x ∈ [xᵢ, xᵢ₊₁]
Lagrange L(x) = Σᵢ yᵢ × Πⱼ≠ᵢ (x−xⱼ)/(xᵢ−xⱼ)
Newton divided difference P(x) = f[x₀] + f[x₀,x₁](x−x₀) + f[x₀,x₁,x₂](x−x₀)(x−x₁) + …
Newton forward difference (equal spacing h, p = (x−x₀)/h) P(x) = y₀ + p·Δy₀ + p(p−1)/2! · Δ²y₀ + p(p−1)(p−2)/3! · Δ³y₀ + …
Natural cubic spline (per segment i) Sᵢ(x) = aᵢ + bᵢ(x−xᵢ) + cᵢ(x−xᵢ)² + dᵢ(x−xᵢ)³    [C² continuous, tridiagonal solve]
Method Continuity What you hear Weakness
Linear C⁰ Straight segments — abrupt turns at each note, mechanical Not smooth; derivative jumps at knots
Lagrange C∞ on interval Musical with few notes; wild pitch swings with many — Runge's phenomenon is audible Unbounded oscillation between points at high degree
Newton DD same as Lagrange Identical sound — same polynomial, built incrementally from a divided-difference table Same Runge issues; table display is the pedagogical value
Newton FD/BD same as Lagrange Identical sound; requires equal spacing between notes Only valid for equally spaced data; auto-detected by the app
Cubic Spline C² Continuous acceleration in pitch — closest to a real musician's portamento Natural boundary condition sets second derivative = 0 at endpoints

Runge's phenomenon

Where the global polynomial breaks

Extend the same Lagrange polynomial slightly past the first and last notes and it swings far outside the played pitch range. Using Chebyshev-spaced nodes (denser near the ends) pulls the oscillation back in — a node placement strategy as important as the method choice itself.

Chebyshev nodes on [−1, 1] (optimal node placement for Lagrange) xₖ = cos( (2k+1)π / (2n+2) ),    k = 0, 1, …, n
Equally spaced — visible overshoot past the pitch range
Chebyshev-spaced — overshoot dramatically reduced

Audio engine

From curve to instrument

The pitch-time curve doesn't drive a synthesizer — it drives a real recording. Each interpolated value becomes a pitch-shift delta applied to a sampled instrument note via the WebAudioFont slides API.

❌ Oscillator engine (audio.ts)

Sine/sawtooth oscillators shaped with ADSR, formants, and unison. Every instrument converges toward the same synthetic tone regardless of tuning. Kept as an offline educational fallback only.

✓ Sample engine (sampler.ts)

Real FluidR3_GM recordings — piano, violin, flute, sax, guitar, clarinet, synth brass — loaded from the WebAudioFontData CDN. Bow attack, body resonance, and harmonic evolution come from the recording itself, not from ADSR curves.

Two playback modes

Play NotesPlays each control point as a separate staccato note — one queueWaveTable call per point, sustainOverride forces a crisp short envelope.
Play GlidePlays a single sustained note that pitch-shifts continuously following the full interpolation curve via the slides array. Uses glideSustain override to hold volume for breath/bowed instruments.

Takeaways

What this project shows

  • Numerical methods are not abstract — they have real, perceivable consequences when applied to sound.
  • Cubic spline wins both ways: C² continuity mathematically, natural-sounding portamento perceptually.
  • Runge's phenomenon is real and audible — not just a warning in a textbook.
  • Node spacing matters as much as method choice: Chebyshev nodes dramatically reduce overshoot.
  • MIDI exists because pitch perception is logarithmic: it linearises the perceptual frequency scale.
  • Real samples beat synthesis: instrument identity lives in the recording, not in ADSR curves.
